**Show the app/college overview:**

“My project is a trust planner for a fictional ten-million-dollar family trust. College comes first, then adult minimum support, while the adult goal is flexible. The children begin at ages eight, six, four and two.

During development, I became more focused on whether users could understand what a number meant and whether the trust could actually meet payments. A portfolio return alone does not answer that question. We separated starting holdings from proposed weights, clarified that college money comes from the same trust, and added standard and custom portfolio cases.

One AI-generated output I personally checked was the college overview. I changed inflation and compared the per-child future-dollar totals with the payment calendar. The calendar updated while the per-child column lagged or failed. I reported that inconsistency. The corrected version sums the same nominal calendar entries for each child instead of relying on a separate display summary. Regression checks now cover that issue and stale responses.

The project now connects portfolio assumptions with monthly cash flows and a clear Evidence dashboard. It also distinguishes saving settings from exporting calculated results.

For the locked experiment, I wrote my prediction before the run and it was committed publicly. Annual inflation changed from two to four percent, with everything else unchanged. College payments rose from about1.088million to1.474million, and adaptive ending purchasing power fell substantially. My suggestion that Treasury returns would automatically rise was not supported: nominal return assumptions stayed fixed in this model.

My decision is to adopt this as a research-monitoring process, with zero percent live allocation. The calculations are useful for comparing assumptions, but a short historical sample compounded for seventy-eight years is not a reliable wealth forecast. Before investing, I would need stronger prospective evidence and verified reserve liquidity.”
