# Trust purpose and payment policy — agreed direction

AI-assisted mandate drafting: Codex organized the student's stated priorities; the analysis and decision are the student's. Educational and research purposes only; not investment or other professional advice. Historical results and projections do not guarantee future outcomes.

Status: user-confirmed policy direction on November 17, 2026, with college duration, semester proration, model-start CPI basis and optional adult-support controls confirmed on November 23, 2026. ## Purpose

The trust seeks to remain a financially healthy lifelong support system for its beneficiaries, funding college as its highest-priority distribution obligation and providing sustainable adult support as a secondary target. Long-term funding health and investment risk limits take priority over pursuing the return target or paying the full adult target when doing so would impair the trust.

## Confirmed policy decisions

- College payments are the number-one payment priority. The starting annual allowance remains $50,000, now CPI-linked. Entry defaults to the first August 1 on or after the child turns 18, with an editable first fall year; degree length is individually adjustable. Funding continues until graduation, including degrees longer than four years. An academic year with only one semester of college is prorated to half the applicable CPI-linked annual allowance. Each semester is paid at its start; fall payments occur August 1 and spring payments January 1, giving alternating five- and seven-month gaps. Past semester dates are excluded from the remaining obligation, with payments due on model start included. Monthly CPI-U updates are confirmed below; future CPI paths remain scenario assumptions.
- User confirmed November 23 that college support is a FIXED ALLOWANCE, not reimbursement of actual eligible expenses. The annual allowance is split between semesters under the agreed schedule; there is no expense-claim input or reimbursement reconciliation requirement.
- The protected college reserve must cover ALL remaining college payments for every beneficiary through graduation, including a five-year degree or any other selected duration. It is not limited to a rolling two- or four-year window. The app reports the full scheduled obligation in model-start dollars. User subsequently confirmed the accounting reserve uses the FULL UNDISCOUNTED remaining allowance in purchasing-power terms, re-indexed with CPI. Required backing assets, eligibility and liquidity remain to be defined; the user has not required all future nominal amounts to be held as idle cash today.
- Full adult distributions of $200,000 annually per eligible beneficiary are a TARGET, not an unconditional obligation to pay that full amount.
- Adult support has a HARD MINIMUM with a starting amount of $20,000 annually per eligible beneficiary, CPI-linked, and may not be suspended entirely. Once all four qualify, the combined starting-dollar minimum is $80,000 annually; its nominal value increases with CPI. This is a required payment policy, not a guarantee that investment assets can always fund it.
- The adult target starts at $200,000 annually per eligible beneficiary and is CPI-linked. It may be reduced when necessary, but not below the CPI-linked minimum. Eligibility now begins at COLLEGE GRADUATION, replacing the fixed age-22 rule. Each child has a separate degree-length input in half-year increments (for example 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5 years). The first adult payment is one month after graduation, followed by monthly payments. Graduation is initially estimated as May 31 after a spring final semester or December 31 after a fall final semester; it is editable within that final funded semester. Extend the degree to fund additional semesters. The first adult payment is one month after the actual selected graduation date, clamped when the target month is shorter.
- After college funding, maintaining sufficient funding for future support is the highest priority. Principal may fluctuate or decline; neither preserving the initial nominal balance nor preserving its original purchasing power is a separate hard constraint.
- Evaluate funding until the YOUNGEST CHILD REACHES AGE 80, confirmed November 23. With the starting ages 8, 6, 4 and 2, this is a 78-year / 936-month planning horizon, at which the children are 86, 84, 82 and 80. This is the analysis cutoff, not a termination of the lifetime-support objective or proof of solvency beyond the cutoff. Permission for principal to decline does not waive risk limits or authorize uncontrolled depletion. Adult funding-health tests and reduction/restoration rules remain open; the API reports this horizon dynamically from the selected youngest age, but a funding engine is not yet implemented.
- Take the investment risk limit seriously; do not increase permitted risk solely to sustain adult distributions or reach the 10% return target.
- The existing 25% volatility limit and 33% turnover limit have not been numerically amended; calculation definitions and breach responses remain to be settled.
- All active benefits are expressed in model-start purchasing power and indexed from model start, including the years before the first benefit payment. This applies to college, adult minimum and adult target. Benefits fall proportionately when CPI falls; the adult floor is a purchasing-power floor, not a fixed nominal-dollar floor. User confirmed MONTHLY CPI-U updates: U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted; use the latest published observation actually available at model start and at each update date. Do not treat an observation month as its release date or use unpublished future values. User subsequently confirmed first-day monthly updates before payments using only vintages published before that day; baseline excludes same-day model-start publications. The baseline applies until the next first-day reset. These cutoffs are implemented in the keyless CPI reserve lookup. Future CPI is optional and explicitly a user scenario.
- Reassess the adult-support level MONTHLY, separately from monthly CPI indexing. This review decides whether payments should remain at the goal or move toward the CPI-linked minimum. Quantitative affordability tests, cut/restoration formula and sharing across beneficiaries remain open; monthly review frequency alone does not approve a specific reduction rule.
- Adult support is toggleable in the app, with editable annual minimum and goal amounts per eligible child. College allowance is also editable. The Project 2 reference case retains $50,000 college allowance, adult support enabled, a $20,000 adult minimum and a $200,000 adult target. A college-only setting or changed payment amounts is an explicitly labelled alternative scenario, not evidence of passing the reference trust mandate. Turning adult support off removes its obligations; it is not a payment-reduction rule within the reference case.

- How are investment risk and turnover measured, reviewed and handled after a breach?

- The primary planning horizon is settled at youngest age 80. Define funding-health thresholds, terminal-payment accounting and any longer-life stress case. A finite projection does not prove lifelong solvency. ## Measurement caution

A volatility limit controls a measure of return variability. It does not by itself prevent large losses, cash shortfalls, or depletion caused by withdrawals. Funding tests and payment-adjustment rules must be evaluated separately from portfolio volatility. If protected obligations cannot be funded under the adopted rules, the app must flag the shortfall rather than silently waive the risk limit, cut college payments, reduce adult support below its $20,000 floor, or assume higher returns. If college obligations and minimum adult payments cannot both be funded, flag mandate failure and escalate; no resolution to that conflict has been preauthorized.
